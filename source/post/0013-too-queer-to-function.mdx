## It's All A Big Mess

I had this cute idea of making the diff, then I thought: I know, I'll now write some thoughts on each of those changes. Like a log. Neat. Easy. Gender, sexuality, neurodivergence, etc. All in their own little box.

Except it's really not like that at all, at least not for me.

I couldn't allow myself to look at men in any romantic or sexual capacity until I was at a comfortable distance from my own masculinity. Even now, being perceived as a (gay) man would be very uncomfortable. If I didn't have this autistic perspective on social norms, I might have not thought nearly as much about gender, sexuality and relationship dynamics and I may very likely have just accepted myself as a monogamous trans lesbian.

## What Even Is A Gender?

Masculinity always felt like an icky identity to have. I never did fit in, but I never didn't fit in enough to be visibly outside. Part of it was masking and holding back for sure, but honestly neither gender feels really "at home" to me. I do enjoy performing femininity in some contexts. It's fun, playful. It can be sexy and enjoyable. But it does feel like a performance.

I do enjoy having a feminine-looking body though, that is for certain. I like having boobs and curves. But I don't really "feel" like either of the binary genders. I actually don't feel like any gender, not when I'm all by myself. When I'm just me, I'm no gender at all. I might not even precisely be me when I'm on my own.

It's hard to describe how I got here. I don't think there's been a clear moment. It happened over time. I just met more queer people. Spending time around people that feel safe definitely was a big part of it. Not having to perform "one of the two genders" and just being a little bit more comfortable being just myself helped a lot.
